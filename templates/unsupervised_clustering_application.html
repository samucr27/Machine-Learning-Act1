K-Means Clustering — Application



Problem Context

Shopping malls want to understand their customers beyond a single average profile, so they can design marketing strategies that actually match how different groups of shoppers behave. The phenomenon analyzed here is customer segmentation. Each row in the dataset represents one mall customer, described by their annual income and their spending score. Unlike the classification models used elsewhere in this project, clustering does not rely on a labeled target variable. Instead, the algorithm discovers natural groupings in the data on its own, based purely on how similar customers are to one another. The value the model attempts to discover is the group, or cluster, each customer naturally belongs to.

Annual Income and Spending Score were chosen because together they capture both a customer's purchasing power and their actual buying behavior. Income alone only shows what a customer could spend, while Spending Score alone only shows how much they do spend, without context. Combining both variables reveals meaningful patterns, such as customers with high income but low spending, or customers with modest income but high spending, that a single variable could never expose on its own.

Data Preprocessing

Before training, the raw dataset was checked for missing values. {{ dataset_info.n_dropped }} records containing null values were removed, leaving {{ dataset_info.n_records }} clean records for clustering. Because Annual Income and Spending Score are measured on very different scales (income in thousands of dollars, spending score from 1 to 100), both variables were standardized using scikit-learn's StandardScaler before training. This step is essential for K-Means, since the algorithm groups points based on distance, and a variable with a larger numeric range would otherwise dominate the distance calculation and distort the clusters.

K-Means Model Configuration

A KMeans model from scikit-learn was trained on the scaled Annual Income and Spending Score values, following this pipeline:

CSV Dataset → Pandas → Drop Nulls → Feature Scaling → K-Means Training → Cluster Assignment → Evaluation → Visualization

The model was configured with K = {{ dataset_info.n_clusters }} clusters. This number was chosen because it produces well-separated, evenly sized groups that map cleanly onto distinct customer profiles (for example, high income with low spending, or low income with high spending), rather than splitting the data into groups too small to act on or merging genuinely different customer types into one.

Cluster Interpretation

Reading the centroids above against the scatter plot reveals distinct customer profiles. Clusters with high income and high spending score represent premium customers who both can and do spend heavily — strong candidates for loyalty programs and exclusive offers. Clusters with high income but low spending score represent cautious, high-earning shoppers who are not currently engaging with the mall — good targets for personalized promotions designed to convert their spending potential into actual purchases. Clusters with low income but high spending score represent budget-conscious customers who are already highly engaged, making them receptive to value-driven, frequent promotions. Clusters near average income and average spending score represent typical, stable shoppers who respond well to general, mall-wide marketing rather than targeted campaigns.

Model Evaluation — Silhouette Score

The clustering was evaluated using the silhouette score, which measures how well each customer fits within its assigned cluster compared to the next closest cluster. The score ranges from -1 to 1: values close to 1 indicate dense, well-separated clusters, values near 0 indicate overlapping clusters, and negative values indicate customers that may have been assigned to the wrong cluster. The model achieved a silhouette score of {{ dataset_info.silhouette_score | round(4) }}, which indicates {% if dataset_info.silhouette_score >= 0.5 %} strong, well-separated clustering, meaning customers within each group are clearly more similar to one another than to customers in other groups.
