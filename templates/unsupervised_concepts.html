Unsupervised Machine Learning — Concepts



What is Unsupervised Learning?

Unsupervised learning trains a model on data that has no labels. The algorithm never sees a "correct answer" during training, unlike supervised learning (used in Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, and Extra Trees). Instead, it looks for structure in the data on its own: groups of similar records, patterns, or hidden relationships. It is useful when the goal is to explore data and discover structure, not to predict a known outcome.

What is Clustering?

Clustering is the most common unsupervised task. It groups records into clusters, so that records in the same cluster are similar to each other, and records in different clusters are different. No cluster labels exist beforehand. The algorithm creates them from the data itself.

Grouping customers by purchase behavior or spending patterns, without knowing the groups in advance.

Organizing articles or messages into topics based on their content, with no predefined topic list.

Finding records that do not fit well into any cluster, which often signals an unusual or suspicious case.

How K-Means Works

K-Means is the most widely used clustering algorithm. It splits the data into K clusters, where K is chosen before running the algorithm. It works through a repeated cycle of assignment and update:

1 Choose K, the number of clusters, and place K initial centroids. A centroid is a point that represents the center of a cluster.
2 Calculate the distance from each record to every centroid (usually Euclidean distance), and assign each record to its nearest centroid.
3 Recalculate each centroid as the average position of all records now assigned to it.
4 Repeat steps 2 and 3. Stop once the centroids no longer move, or after a fixed number of iterations.

Example: records reassigned to the nearest centroid, and centroids updated across iterations.

The Role of Centroids

A centroid is the center point of a cluster. It is not necessarily one of the actual data points. It is calculated as the average of every record assigned to that cluster. Centroids drive the whole algorithm: every record is grouped based on which centroid it is closest to, and every centroid is repositioned based on which records belong to it. When the centroids stabilize, the clusters are considered final.

Why Iterations Matter

The first centroids are usually placed randomly or manually, so the first assignment of records to clusters is rarely the best one. Each iteration corrects this: records move to a better fitting cluster, and centroids shift toward the true center of their group. Comparing the variance within each cluster across iterations shows this improvement directly. Variance should decrease as the clusters become tighter and more accurate.
