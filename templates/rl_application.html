Reinforcement Learning — Application

A virtual robot starts at A and has to find its way to the target T on a 10 × 10 map. Nobody tells it the route: it learns it with Q-Learning, by trying actions, receiving rewards and penalties, and updating its estimate of how good each action is in every cell. The map hides walls that block the corridors and Danger Zones placed on the most tempting shortcuts.


Environment (10 × 10)

The state of the agent is its position (row, column), numbered from (0, 0) at the top-left corner. The agent starts at A (0, 0) and the target is at T (9, 9).

Walls (#): they cannot be crossed; the agent stays in place and receives the wall penalty.
Danger Zones (D): the agent can enter them, but every visit is penalized.

Training Parameters

Q(s, a) is estimated with scikit-learn's SGDRegressor: predict() returns the Q-values of a state and partial_fit() updates them after every step. The agent follows an epsilon-greedy strategy: with probability ε it explores a random action, otherwise it exploits the action with the highest Q-value.

Train the Agent

Pressing the button runs the full Q-Learning process: initial state → action selection (exploration / exploitation) → interaction with the environment → reward and next state → Q-value update → next action, repeated until T or the step limit, for every training episode. Then the learned policy is evaluated without exploration. It takes a few seconds.
