K-Means — Manual Exercise

This page walks through the K-Means algorithm step by step on a small sample, before applying it to the full dataset in the Clustering Application. Every distance, assignment, and centroid shown below is calculated by the application from the sample CSV file; nothing is hard-coded.


1. Dataset and Context

The sample contains {{ iterations[0].table | length }} mall customers, taken from the same customer segmentation dataset used in the Clustering Application (store_customers_100_sample.csv). The goal is to discover natural groups of shoppers based on how much they earn and how much they spend, without any predefined labels.

VariableAxisMeaning
Annual Income (k$) X The customer's yearly income, in thousands of US dollars. It represents purchasing power.
Spending Score (1-100) Y A score assigned by the mall from 1 to 100 based on the customer's buying behavior. Higher means the customer spends more.

4. Iterations of the Algorithm

Each iteration repeats the same three steps: (1) calculate the Euclidean distance from every customer to each centroid, (2) assign each customer to the cluster of its nearest centroid, and (3) recalculate each centroid as the average of the customers assigned to it.

d(p, c) = √[ (xp − xc)² + (yp − yc)² ]
new centroid = ( average X of the cluster , average Y of the cluster )

In the plots, the white X markers are the previous centroids and the arrows show where each centroid moved. In the distance tables, the highlighted value is the smallest distance, which decides the cluster the customer is assigned to.

5. Variance Comparison Across Iterations

The within-cluster variance of each cluster is the average squared distance between its customers and its centroid. The total is the sum over the three clusters. A lower value means the customers inside each cluster are closer to each other, so the clusters are more compact.

The manual exercise shows how K-Means works internally: it starts from three chosen customers as centroids, and in each iteration the customers are reassigned and the centroids move toward the center of their groups, reducing the within-cluster variance.
